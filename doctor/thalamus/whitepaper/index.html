Turning a photographed hospital file into context an agent can act on

This brief follows one real patient record end to end. Two files a patient handed over. Six clinical documents hiding inside them, from four asserters across two cities. One hundred and eleven atomic clinical data points out the other side — each one traceable to the sentence it came from. Then it sets out how those points become Relevant Clinical Context: the task-scoped state a vertical voice agent speaks from.

I

What unstructured medical data actually looks like

Caren handed over two files. A JPEG of a teleconsultation prescription, and an eleven-megabyte PDF she photographed page by page under a desk lamp.

That PDF is not a document. It is five unrelated clinical documents, from three different signatories, stacked into one file with no table of contents, no separator, and no shared layout. One page is printed on ECG graph paper and arrives rotated ninety degrees. Another has her thumb in the frame. None of the six pages contains a single character of embedded text — every word on this page of evidence exists only as pixels.

This is the ordinary case, not the hard case. It is what a health record looks like before anybody has done anything to it.

Exhibit A The complete input corpus — 2 files, 6 logical documents, 0 characters of machine-readable text.

Why this defeats a single model call

Handing all six pages to a model and asking for JSON produces something plausible on the first run and something subtly different on the second. Identifiers drift. The same doctor becomes two doctors. A finding quoted in one report gets silently merged with the report that first stated it, and the trail back to the paper is gone. For a spreadsheet of restaurant reviews that is a nuisance. For a medication list it is a safety problem.

II

The unit of work: an atomic clinical data point

An ACDP is one discrete clinical fact, written as a single self-contained sentence, classified against a closed vocabulary, and bound by foreign key to the medication, biomarker, person and document behind it.

Atomic is the operative word. “Avoid alcohol, avoid products containing eucalyptus oil, camphor, essential oils” is one line on a prescription and four separate clinical instructions. Split apart, each becomes independently retrievable, independently checkable, and independently attributable to the doctor who gave it.

Anatomy of one row

Every ACDP carries the same fields, whatever kind of document it came from. Four of them are foreign keys into detail sheets; two are closed enumerations; one is the verbatim evidence.

acdpThe self-contained clinical statement
categoryOne of 16 fixed values — diagnosis, complaints, medication, lifestyle_advice…
body_systemOne of 19 — classifies the content, not the author's speciality
source_spanVerbatim text from the page that substantiates it
asserter_idWho said it — resolved per page, not per file
source_idWhich document it came from
medication_idThe specific drug row, when the point is about a drug
biomarker_idThe specific measurement row, when the point is a value

How determinism is enforced — the model proposes content, code assigns identity

The language model is never allowed to decide what anything is. It transcribes a page and proposes candidate facts through a forced tool-use schema. Everything that could drift between runs is done afterwards in ordinary Python:

  • Identity. Every acdp_id, m001, b001 and asserter number is assigned by a counter in link.py, walking documents in a fixed sort order. The model never emits an ID.
  • Person resolution. Asserters are merged on a normalised name key only. “DR. P.K.” on the EEG and “Dr P.K.” on the discharge summary collapse to one person, even though their printed specialities differ. An unnamed asserter is never merged with another unnamed one — there is no identity signal to merge on, so the ECG gets its own unknown row rather than being quietly attributed to the neurologist.
  • Page-level attribution. A five-page bundle is split into five units before extraction and each ACDP is attributed to whoever signed its page. That is why the ECG's five biomarkers sit under asserter 104 and not under the doctor who signed page three.
  • Dedup. Repeated header lines reprinted on every page of one document collapse to a single row. The identical sentence appearing in two different documents is never collapsed — that is a second assertion by a second author, and the distinction is the whole point.

Same folder in, same workbook out, byte for byte.

How hallucination is caught — verbatim grounding on every single row

Each proposed ACDP must carry a source_span: the exact stretch of document text that substantiates it. After linking, every span is fuzzy-matched back against the page transcript it claims to come from. Anything scoring below 85 on a partial-ratio match is written into the review report rather than trusted.

If the model asserts something the page does not say, the span either cannot be produced or does not match. There is nowhere for an invented fact to hide.

Caren's run flagged five rows out of 111 — all five the benign failure mode where a fact genuinely on the page is split across table cells, so the reconstructed span does not read back as one contiguous string:

acdp score span it claimed
20 75.3 TAB BRIVANEXT (50 MG) (6 1. strips) Brivaracetam Frequency 1 Tab twice a day Duration 4 weeks
21 72.4 TAB CLOBA MT (10 MG) (1 2. strip) Clobazam Frequency 1 Tab as required * to be taken sub lingually
72 61.9 QT/QTc-Baz 356/423 ms
73 68.4 P-R-T axes 55 63 48
88 83.7 she had history of epilepsy at the age of 7 years

Rows 20 and 21 stitch a prescription table back into a sentence. Rows 72 and 73 read values off a rotated ECG corner block. Row 88 fails by 1.3 points on a pronoun change. A human reviewer clears all five in under a minute — and crucially, the system flagged them itself rather than presenting them as certain.

What stops a bad run from shipping

Two classes of problem, handled differently. Blocking failures raise and the run produces no workbook at all: any foreign key that does not resolve to a real row, any empty ACDP text. There is no partial or best-effort output.

Non-blocking uncertainty is collected and written beside the workbook as review.json — weak grounding, dates that would not parse, a category or body system the model could not commit to, a medication or biomarker extracted but never referenced by any statement. Nothing is silently dropped and nothing is silently guessed.

Caren's run: 111 rows, zero broken references, zero unparseable dates, zero orphaned medications or biomarkers, five grounding flags.

III

What the structure buys that a summary cannot

Once the record is a graph of attributed facts rather than a stack of prose, things become visible that are not stated anywhere in any single document.

Each of the four findings below was produced by comparing ACDP rows to each other. None of them can be read off a page. All four matter to anyone treating this patient.

Finding 1 · Corroboration

The same MRI finding, asserted three times by three routes

The bilateral hippocampal FLAIR hyperintensity appears as acdp 29, 30 from the radiologist who read the scan, as acdp 48, 49 where the neurologist quotes it into an outpatient note, and as acdp 18, 19 where a Delhi teleconsultant restates it second-hand six months later.

Three rows, one truth, and the ACDP layer preserves which is primary. A summariser would have merged them into one sentence and lost the ability to tell the radiologist's read from hearsay about it.

Finding 2 · Contradiction

The discharge summary is wrong, and the timestamps explain why

acdp 95 — from the discharge summary — states the MRI “showed no significant intracranial abnormality.” acdp 29, 30 — from the MRI report itself — describe subtle FLAIR hyperintensity in both hippocampi with mild atrophy.

Both are faithfully extracted. Both are in her file. The metadata resolves it: the scan was performed 01 Nov and reported 03 Nov at 13:12. She was discharged 02 Nov at 14:00. The discharge summary was written before the radiologist finished reading the scan, and nobody went back to amend it.

Finding 3 · Drug safety

A documented intolerance that no single document declares

acdp 50–53 record four adverse effects after starting LEV — sleep disturbance, anxiety, shortness of breath, mixing up of words — and the pipeline correctly routed them to three different body systems. The word “intolerance” appears nowhere.

Pair those four rows with the medication timeline and the picture is unambiguous: she was discharged on levetiracetam on 02 Nov, and five days later that had been replaced. Any agent touching this patient needs that as a hard rule, not as a paragraph it might read carefully enough.

Finding 4 · A gap we are being explicit about

One molecule, three rows, two brand names

m001 BRIVANEXT, m003 BREVIPIL and m005 BREVIPIL are all brivaracetam 50 mg twice daily, recorded by three different prescribers. The workbook keeps them as three rows.

That is deliberate at this layer — the ACDP sheet stays faithful to what each document actually said, and does not invent a merge it cannot prove. Collapsing them into one therapy with a start date is a resolution problem, and resolution is the next layer's job — which is what the rest of this brief is about.

IV

Relevant Clinical Context

RCC is a task-scoped, deterministic projection of the ACDP graph, compiled for exactly one vertical agent and nothing else.

It is not a summary of the patient, and it is not a retrieval index over her documents. It is the working state a narrow agent needs in order to hold one specific conversation safely: what is true right now, what it must ask, what it must never say, and what should end the call and page a human.

The vertical matters. An agent that calls epilepsy patients between neurology visits to track seizure frequency and antiseizure-medication adherence needs a different projection from one booking appointments or one reconciling insurance. Building context for “a healthcare assistant” in general means building it for no task in particular — which is how agents end up confidently wrong. RCC is compiled per agent, per task, from the same underlying graph.

Roughly sixty lines. Small enough to sit in an agent's system prompt on every turn, specific enough that the agent never has to reason its way to a safety rule mid-sentence.

Why this is compiled, not retrieved

The obvious alternative is to chunk her six documents into a vector store and let the agent retrieve. On this record, that fails in a way that is easy to demonstrate.

Retrieval over documents

Ask “what antiseizure medication is she on?” The discharge summary is the longest, most authoritative-looking, most quotable chunk in the corpus. It says TAB LEVIPIL 500 MG 1-0-1 CONTINUE.

The agent tells a patient with a documented levetiracetam intolerance to keep taking levetiracetam. Every word of that answer is grounded in a real document. It is still wrong, because the document is eight months stale and the retriever has no notion of supersession.

Compiled RCC

The compiler walks medication ACDPs in date order, applies supersession, resolves three brand strings to one molecule, and reads rows 50–53 as a stop reason.

Levetiracetam does not appear in current_therapy at all. It appears once, in do_not_reintroduce, with its reason and the five rows that prove it. The wrong answer is not something the agent has to be smart enough to avoid — it is not in the context it was given.

Note what the agent never does: reason about her record live. It reads a compiled object. The clinical judgement was made once, deterministically, at compile time — where it can be reviewed, versioned and tested — instead of being re-derived from raw documents on every call.

V

Where we are, and how RCC gets built

Everything through the workbook is running today on real patient folders. Everything from resolution upward is designed and not yet shipped. The stages below are marked honestly.

01
Intake and page splitting
Content-sniffed file typing by magic bytes rather than extension. Every PDF is split to one unit per page, because one file is routinely five unrelated reports.
Shipped
02
Two-phase extraction
A header pass transcribes the page and pulls document metadata, medications and biomarkers. A second pass proposes ACDPs against that transcript through a forced schema, chunked so a long document cannot truncate its own fact list. Provider-agnostic across Vertex, Anthropic and OpenAI.
Shipped
03
Deterministic linking
Code assigns every identifier, merges continuation pages, resolves asserters on a normalised name key, attributes each fact to the page it came from, and votes patient identity across documents rather than trusting one header.
Shipped
04
Validation and review report
Blocking foreign-key integrity, verbatim grounding at threshold 85, date normalisation, orphan detection. Uncertainty is written out for a human instead of being resolved silently.
Shipped
05
Clinical coding
SNOMED CT and ICD-11 columns exist in the workbook and are empty today. Coding is what lets a rule say “any antiseizure medication” rather than matching on strings, and it is the precondition for most of what follows.
In progress
06
Entity resolution across documents
Collapse brand and salt variants of one molecule into a single therapy with a start date and, where it exists, a stop reason. Today the workbook keeps BREVIPIL, BRIVANEXT and Brivaracetam as three rows on purpose; resolving them belongs here, above the faithful layer, not inside it.
Next
07
Temporal resolution
Give every fact an event date and an assertion date, so supersession is computable. This is what turns the discharge-summary contradiction from a conflict into a resolved sequence: reported 03 Nov beats written 02 Nov.
Next
08
Problem-oriented rollup
Group the resolved facts into active problems with their supporting evidence, current therapy, resolved history, and open loops. One patient-level graph, still agent-agnostic.
Next
09
RCC projection per vertical
A declarative spec per agent selects from the graph and compiles the object in Exhibit D — including its guardrails. Deterministic, versioned, diffable between runs, and re-compiled whenever a new document lands.
Next
10
Evaluation harness
Clinician-labelled gold RCCs per vertical, scored on the things that matter: is any current medication missing, is any contraindication absent, does any guardrail fail to fire. A projection is only shippable once it passes its own suite.

What is true today, stated plainly

Working

  • A command-line pipeline that takes a patient folder and returns a six-sheet workbook plus a review report.
  • Photographed, rotated, skewed and native-digital documents, all handled through the same path.
  • Deterministic identity, references and dedup — same folder in, same workbook out.
  • Verbatim source grounding on every row, with sub-threshold matches surfaced rather than buried.
  • Swappable model provider, so quality and cost can be tuned without touching the schema.

Not yet

  • RCC itself. Exhibits D and E are the design target, compiled by hand from the real workbook for this brief.
  • Clinical coding — the SNOMED and ICD-11 columns are empty.
  • Cross-document medication resolution, as described in Finding 4.
  • Bundled-PDF metadata — a five-report file currently takes its category and date from its first page.
  • Review is a JSON file, not an interface. One patient per run, no database behind it, not yet wired into the consumer app.

The bet

The hard part of clinical AI is not generating fluent medical language. It is knowing, with proof, what is actually true about one patient right now — and being able to show where each piece of that came from when someone asks. Thalamus builds that layer first, deterministically, and treats everything an agent says as a projection of it rather than an inference over raw paper.
